Every lighthouse has its own rhythm of flashes, so sailors can tell them apart. Here each one blinks its real signal. Hover one to see its pattern; pinch or scroll to zoom.

What's on the map. named, working lighthouses in the waters of the 50 states, each blinking the signal printed on NOAA's nautical charts, at real speed. Each starts at a random point in its cycle, since real lighthouses aren't in sync.

Where the data comes from. Lights and their signals: , every light on the general, coastal and harbor charts. Lighthouse names: OpenStreetMap lighthouses and NOAA's charted landmarks.

How they're matched. A named lighthouse gets the light that is seen farthest out to sea within 300 m of it. A light drawn on several charts counts once.

Left out. Airport beacons and obstruction lights; front/rear range lights, numbered pier and channel lights, and buoys; ruins and retired towers with no working light; lights in other countries' waters; Puerto Rico and Pacific territories, which this map projection doesn't show. NOAA's approach-scale charts wouldn't download, so a few lighthouses only charted there may be missing.
